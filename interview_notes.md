# Simulated Interview Notes for ReflectBlocks

These notes are synthetic examples created for drafting and analysis practice.
They should not be presented as real participant transcripts unless they are
replaced with actual collected data. The imagined study context is Bangladesh.
All interview conversation was conducted in Bangla; responses below are written
as English translations/paraphrases.

## Interview 1: P02

Participant profile: Parent, above age 45, lives in Dhaka, journals on and off
using a notes app on their phone.

Session context: In-person home interview, about 20 minutes, conducted in
Bangla and translated into English for these notes. No audio was recorded; the
researcher took written notes during and immediately after the session.
Participant completed both the interview and the prompt-card exercise.

### Interview Excerpt

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you wrote about something that
happened to you.

**P02:** Two days ago, after an argument with my brother about a family
matter. I was angry and wanted to get it out of my head, so I opened my notes
app and just typed quickly.

**Interviewer:** What happened from the moment you had the thought to when you
wrote it down?

**P02:** I did not wait long that time. I was still upset, so I wrote while it
was fresh. That part was easy. It was afterward that felt strange.

**Interviewer:** What felt strange?

**P02:** The app showed me a question, something like "what did you learn from
this." I did not want to answer that. I was still angry, I just wanted to vent,
not think of a lesson yet. It felt like the app was rushing me to be calm
before I was ready.

**Interviewer:** How did that make you feel about the prompts in general?

**P02:** Some prompts are fine, even helpful, like asking what happened or how
I felt. But when I am in the middle of venting and it asks me to reflect and
learn, it feels like homework, like the app is grading me. Other days, if I am
calmer, the same question does not bother me at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you want instead, in that moment?

**P02:** A way to just skip it with one tap, not go into a settings menu to
turn it off. If I have to dig through settings while I am angry, I will just
close the app instead.

**Interviewer:** How did you react to the prompt cards?

**P02:** "What happened?" and "How did you feel?" are good, I would answer
those most days. "What did you learn?" is good only later, not right away.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the system to respect?

**P02:** That I am not always ready to be reflective the second I start
writing. Let me vent first if I need to.

### Card Exercise Notes

Useful prompts:

- What happened?
- How did you feel?

Context-dependent or disliked prompts:

- What did you learn? (only wanted later, not while venting)

Participant comment: Wants a fast, single-action way to dismiss a prompt that
does not fit the moment, without opening a settings screen.

### Initial Codes

- Writes quickly while emotion is fresh
- Some prompts feel like an assignment when venting
- "What did you learn" prompt timing matters
- Wants low-friction dismiss, not a settings toggle
- Mood changes which prompts feel acceptable

### Link to Themes

- T2: Prompts help, but only sometimes.

## Interview 2: P06

Participant profile: Elderly relative, above age 45, lives in Dhaka, writes
daily using a fixed template in a notebook.

Session context: In-person home interview, about 20 minutes, conducted in
Bangla and translated into English for these notes. No audio was recorded; the
researcher took written notes during and immediately after the session.
Participant completed both the interview and the prompt-card exercise.

### Interview Excerpt

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you wrote about something that
happened to you.

**P06:** Every evening I write a little, it is my habit. Yesterday I wrote
about a visit from my grandchildren.

**Interviewer:** What happened from the moment you had the thought to when you
wrote it down?

**P06:** For me this is not usually difficult, I already have my fixed
questions I answer every day. I write my answers in Bangla, in my own words.

**Interviewer:** What about a feature that turns your written answers into a
complete, organized entry?

**P06:** That is where I would be careful. If a system takes what I wrote and
changes it into something smoother, I want to know exactly which words are
mine and which words it added or changed. Even a small change, I want to see
it clearly, not have to go looking for it.

**Interviewer:** Why does that matter to you?

**P06:** Because it is my reflection, my memory of the moment. If the system
quietly changes my meaning, even a little, it is no longer really my entry. I
do not mind it organizing things, but it should be honest about what it did.

**Interviewer:** Would you want that shown automatically, or only if you asked
for it?

**P06:** Automatically, by default. I should not have to search for a button
or a special screen to find out what changed. It should just be visible when I
look at the entry.

**Interviewer:** Would you still want to edit the entry afterward?

**P06:** Yes, every sentence, even the ones it generated. If something is
wrong or does not sound like me, I want to be able to fix it right away.

**Interviewer:** How did you react to the prompt cards?

**P06:** I like having the same questions every day, that is already my habit.
I did not need new ones.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the system to respect?

**P06:** My own words. Whatever it adds, I want to see the line between what I
wrote and what it wrote.

### Card Exercise Notes

Useful prompts:

- Fixed daily questions (already part of participant's own routine)

Participant comment: Less interested in new prompt variety; more concerned
with source-transparency for any generated or reorganized text.

### Initial Codes

- Prefers fixed template, daily habit
- Wants clear separation of own words vs. system-generated text
- Wants source-transparency visible by default, not an inspectable extra
- Wants every sentence editable after generation
- Sees unmarked changes as a loss of authorship

### Link to Themes

- T3: People want to know what the system changed.

## Interview 3: P05

Participant profile: Parent, above age 45, lives in Dhaka, writes daily using
a fixed template.

Session context: In-person home interview, about 20 minutes, conducted in
Bangla and translated into English for these notes. No audio was recorded; the
researcher took written notes during and immediately after the session.
Participant completed both the interview and the prompt-card exercise.

### Interview Excerpt

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you wrote about something that
happened to you.

**P05:** Last weekend my sister's family came to visit, the children stayed
two nights. I wrote about it the same evening, same as always.

**Interviewer:** What happened from the moment you had the thought to when you
wrote it down?

**P05:** For me it is not really a separate thought and then a delay, it is
just part of my evening. I always answer the same few questions before I
sleep: what happened, who was there, how I felt. It takes a few minutes.

**Interviewer:** Why do you keep the same questions every day instead of
changing them?

**P05:** Because these are the things I want to remember later. Small family
moments, who visited, what my grandchildren said, a joke at dinner. If I do
not write it the same day, in a few months I only remember that something nice
happened, not the details. The fixed questions make sure I do not skip the
details that matter to me.

**Interviewer:** Have you tried other prompts or a blank page instead?

**P05:** I do not need new prompts. The same ones work because I already know
what I want to capture: family experiences, so I can read them again later, or
so my children can read them one day.

**Interviewer:** How did you react to the prompt cards?

**P05:** I liked seeing the same kind of questions I already use. I would not
want the system to change them on me or suggest different ones each time,
that would break my habit.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the system to respect?

**P05:** That I already have a routine that works for me. Let me keep my fixed
questions every time, not push new ones just because they might suit someone
else.

### Card Exercise Notes

Useful prompts:

- Same fixed set every day (what happened, who was there, how I felt)

Participant comment: Does not want prompt variety; wants the same template to
persist by default across sessions, since consistency is what makes the
family record useful later.

### Initial Codes

- Uses a fixed daily template, not spontaneous capture
- Motivation: preserving small family experiences and details before they fade
- Wants the same prompts to persist as a personal default
- Resists prompt variety or system-suggested changes to the routine
- Sees the entries as a record for self and family to reread later

### Link to Themes

- T2: Prompts help, but only sometimes. (counterexample: fixed default preferred)
