# Simulated Interview Notes for ReflectBlocks

These notes are synthetic examples created for drafting and analysis practice.
They should not be presented as real participant transcripts unless they are
replaced with actual collected data. The imagined study context is Bangladesh.
All interview conversation was conducted in Bangla; responses below are written
as English translations/paraphrases.

## Interview 1: P03

Participant profile: Parent, above age 45, lives in Dhaka, journals
occasionally in a notebook and sometimes leaves short notes in a phone notes
app.

Session context: In-person home interview, about 20 minutes, conducted in
Bangla and translated into English for these notes. No audio was recorded; the
researcher took written notes during and immediately after the session.
Participant completed both the interview and the prompt-card exercise.

### Interview Excerpt

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you wrote about something that
happened to you at home, work, or while moving around the city.

**P03:** It was after a family gathering after Jummah lunch. I felt bothered by
something someone said, but I did not want to react in front of everyone. Later
that night I wrote two or three lines in my notebook.

**Interviewer:** What happened from the moment you first had the thought to
when you wrote it down?

**P03:** The thought came while I was washing dishes after everyone left. At
that time I knew exactly what I wanted to say. But by the time I sat with the
notebook, after evening prayer and household work, I forgot the exact feeling.
I remembered the event, but not the sharpness of it.

**Interviewer:** What made it difficult to start?

**P03:** Opening the notebook feels like I have to write properly. I usually
write in Bangla there, and if I only write one messy sentence, it feels
unfinished. So sometimes I delay it, and then the thought is gone.

**Interviewer:** What do you usually do when that happens?

**P03:** I sometimes write a sentence in my phone, like "felt ignored today."
But later I do not always understand what I meant. It needs context. Also, at
home people may borrow my phone for a call, so I do not want the note to open
in front of others.

**Interviewer:** Would a short first-capture step help?

**P03:** Yes, if it is really quick. I do not want to choose categories first.
Just one line, maybe in Bangla or mixed Bangla-English, then later I can expand
it.

**Interviewer:** How did you react to the prompt cards?

**P03:** "What happened?" and "How did you feel?" are useful. "What did you
learn?" is useful only later. If I am still upset, that question feels too
early, like someone is telling me to be patient before I have explained myself.

**Interviewer:** What would you want the system to respect?

**P03:** Privacy. In our homes there are often other people nearby, and I write
things I would not say out loud. Also, if the system changes my Bangla or mixed
language writing into a nice English paragraph, I want to see what came from me
and what it added.

### Card Exercise Notes

Useful prompts:

- What happened?
- How did you feel?
- Why did it affect you?

Less useful or context-dependent prompts:

- What did you learn?
- What would you do differently?

Participant comment: Prompts should appear after the first sentence, not before.

### Initial Codes

- Thought appears during household activity
- Delay causes emotional detail to fade
- Blank notebook creates pressure to write properly
- Phone note preserves trigger but lacks context
- Shared phone/home context raises privacy concern
- Bangla and mixed-language writing matter
- Prompts useful when timed correctly
- Wants source visibility for generated text

### Link to Themes

- T1: People lose the thought before they even start writing.
- T2: Prompts help, but only sometimes.
- T3: People want to know what the system changed.

## Interview 2: P08

Participant profile: Elderly relative, above age 60, lives outside Dhaka, used
to keep a paper diary but stopped recently. Prefers private writing and is
skeptical of AI-generated text.

Session context: In-person home interview, about 20 minutes, conducted in
Bangla and translated into English for these notes. No audio was recorded; the
researcher took written notes during and immediately after the session.
Participant completed the interview but did not want to write full answers on
the prompt cards.

### Interview Excerpt

**Interviewer:** Tell me about the last time you wanted to write something down
but did not. It could be about family, health, work, or any ordinary day.

**P08:** It happens often. I think about writing at night after dinner, when
the house becomes quiet. But then I feel too tired, or the electricity goes and
comes back. By morning it is no longer the same feeling.

**Interviewer:** What did you do instead?

**P08:** Nothing, usually. Sometimes I remember only that I was upset, but not
why it mattered. If I write a small note in Bangla, I understand it better
later than if I force myself to write in English.

**Interviewer:** Would reflection prompts help in that situation?

**P08:** Maybe one or two. Too many questions would make it feel like an exam,
like filling a government form. If I see five boxes, I may close it.

**Interviewer:** Which prompt card felt most useful?

**P08:** "What happened?" That is simple. "How did you feel?" is also okay if
I can answer in my own words. "What did you learn?" I did not like. Not every
day teaches a lesson.

**Interviewer:** What about a feature that turns your block answers into a
complete journal entry?

**P08:** I would not use that. I do not want a machine writing my diary. It can
keep my words organized, but it should not pretend to know my meaning,
especially if I wrote in Bangla and it changes the tone.

**Interviewer:** Would it help if the final entry showed which block each
sentence came from?

**P08:** It is better, but I still prefer a blank page. Maybe younger people in
Dhaka who use apps for everything will like the generated version. For me, the
important thing is that I can write freely and delete everything.

**Interviewer:** What privacy controls would you expect?

**P08:** It should tell me if my writing goes anywhere outside my phone. In the
family, people sometimes help each other with phones, so private writing should
not appear suddenly. If I delete it, I expect it to be gone. I should not need
to read a long policy.

### Card Exercise Notes

Useful prompts:

- What happened?
- How did you feel?

Rejected or disliked prompts:

- What did you learn?
- What would you do differently?

Participant comment: The blank-writing option should be as easy to find as the
guided option.

### Initial Codes

- Reflection delayed until emotion fades
- Too many prompts feel like an exam
- Bangla writing feels more natural for personal reflection
- Rejects generated diary prose
- Wants blank writing as primary option
- Shared household device use affects privacy expectations
- Deletion must be visible and understandable
- Wants plain-language explanation of data movement

### Link to Themes

- T1: People lose the thought before they even start writing.
- T2: Prompts help, but only sometimes.
- T3: People want to know what the system changed.
